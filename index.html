I apply decision-analytic methods, health economics, and real-world evidence to inform policy and strategy across oncology and global health.

As Director of US RWE & Health Outcomes, Oncology at GlaxoSmithKline, I lead real-world evidence generation and health outcomes research supporting oncology portfolio decisions in the US market. My work sits at the intersection of economic modelling, clinical evidence synthesis, and payer strategy.

Before my current role, I spent nearly four years at Bristol Myers Squibb, most recently as Associate Director of WWHEOR Oncology, leading health economics research across the thoracic portfolio (NSCLC, SCLC, mesothelioma). Before industry, I spent six years at the University of Washington's CHOICE Institute, where I completed my Ph.D. and later served as Senior Fellow. My research focused on economic evaluation of health interventions in low- and middle-income countries — Uganda, Malawi, and sub-Saharan Africa broadly — spanning HIV prevention, maternal health, schizophrenia, and supply chain policy.

I trained originally as a pharmacist at Makerere University (Kampala) and hold a Master's in Clinical Pharmacy from the University of Strathclyde (Glasgow). Prior to the US, I was a Lecturer at Mbarara University of Science and Technology.

Demand for Medical Male Circumcision: Theory-Driven Preference HeterogeneityIn preparation
Extends my doctoral dissertation research · Fishing communities, Mukono district, Uganda
This project returns to the discrete choice experiment I fielded for my doctoral dissertation (406 men, 4,872 choice tasks, 13 attributes), first presented at ISPOR 2018. The new work completes the microeconomic framework I began in the dissertation, a two-period model of the decision to circumcise, and uses it to decide which preference correlations to model rather than leaving that choice to the data. Theory-driven, data-driven and fully correlated mixed logit specifications are estimated alongside conditional logit and latent class models, and compared on what each implies for demand-side and supply-side policy. A second paper will extend the framework with behavioural factors and integrated choice and latent variable models.

VOI Explorer: The Value of Longer Follow-Up in Survival ExtrapolationIn progress
R/Shiny application · Abstract in preparation for SMDM 2027
Health technology assessments often rely on survival curves extrapolated from immature trial data, and plausible models can disagree widely about the long term. The VOI Explorer treats that uncertainty as a value-of-information problem. It estimates the expected value of perfect information (EVPI) at successive data cuts, so the decision uncertainty that longer follow-up removes can be expressed in life-months per patient and in population dollar terms. The engine averages across parametric and mixture-cure model families, uses a recentred bootstrap to separate parameter uncertainty from model bias, projects how EVPI falls as the remaining events accrue, and identifies the part of the uncertainty that further follow-up of the same trial cannot resolve. A cross-trial mode adds a correction for differences between trials. The planned case study uses the published data cuts of CheckMate 067 in advanced melanoma, so projections made from early cuts can be checked against later ones.
